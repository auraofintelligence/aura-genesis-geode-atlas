DAY 11 / Learning & reasoning

Attention pacing and learning conditions

Today's objective is to understand that selection well enough to improve a learning encounter.

Open the complete source reading ＋

Attention connects the world available to a person with the part they notice and use. Today's objective is to understand that selection well enough to improve a learning encounter.

The learner compares the same task under different conditions, then designs a pacing arrangement that suits their own needs and the adults they teach. The task is deliberately ordinary: learn a short interface route, follow a melody, interpret a diagram or use a new tool.

The learner identifies the cues, prior knowledge and resources needed. They record the setting, time available, distractions and their initial confidence. A second attempt changes one relevant condition, such as the amount of explanation, display format or time between steps.

The studio task produces a personal learning-condition map. Casual pacing offers longer pauses and fewer simultaneous elements. Normal pacing presents a steady sequence with review points. Fast pacing increases tempo where the learner welcomes it. Additional settings offer captions, spoken alternatives, reduced visual density, assisted communication, sensory adjustment and extra processing time.

The learner tests these options rather than relying only on a label. Technical builders measure observable interaction: completion time, step errors, requests for help and successful return to the task. Creative learners examine the effect of rhythm, imagery and story. Teachers ask the learner which condition felt useful and why. The personal account sits beside the task observations, making the map richer than a performance score. The life reflection returns to an experience of learning with pleasure and an experience of learning under pressure.

The learner identifies how the exterior setting, inner dialogue and available support changed their response. They then select one practice to carry into the next week, such as a visible agenda, a short rehearsal or an accessible route back to help. The earlier life-simulation paper includes available time, learning hours, daily routines, focus and contextual influences. This edition develops those ideas through observed learning conditions and selected support rather than asking a person to estimate an abstract thought-efficiency factor. [S10, pp. 2-5, 13-15] Teaching practice adapts yesterday's lesson for a different learner.

The trainee asks what matters, offers format and pacing choices, then observes the encounter. A peer compares the two versions and identifies which change improved access. Evidence includes the task conditions, learning map, comparison observations and revised lesson. The capability built today will also inform later simulations: the world contains many cues, while a learner or character attends to a selected part of them.

The learner also identifies a condition that helped them sustain curiosity rather than merely finish. That observation shapes a preferred learning rhythm for the coming technical work, connecting task performance with the life they want to inhabit.

DAY 12 / Learning & reasoning

Recall memory and the right context

Today's objective is to distinguish finding a record from interpreting it, then build a retrieval route that preserves context.

Open the complete source reading ＋

A useful Aura retrieves the information relevant to the encounter in front of it. Today's objective is to distinguish finding a record from interpreting it, then build a retrieval route that preserves context.

The learner compares a title search, a category route, a linked memory and a model-assisted search using their own selected collection.

The starting question is concrete. A learner planning a creative performance may need a song draft, the reason it was written, a previous audience response and a practical equipment note. A learner discussing a friendship may need the relevant conversation and the conditions under which it occurred. The collection's relationships help the Aura bring the right context together.

The studio task creates six retrieval questions. Some name the item directly; others describe its meaning; another asks for a relationship across categories.

The learner records what a suitable response would include before running the searches. A technical partner examines indexing, metadata and semantic representations. A nontechnical partner checks whether the returned material answers the actual question.

The learner inspects each response for missing context, irrelevant content and confusion between versions. A revised interpretation of a memory is linked to its earlier record, allowing the current question to receive the appropriate account. Permissions filter the material available to a particular assistant or audience. A teaching demonstration uses only the records selected for that purpose. The October alignment source separates recall, training and action. Recall brings chosen information into an exchange. Training changes a model's learned patterns. Action produces a result through an available tool. Today's lesson concentrates on recall and prepares those later operations by making the retrieval boundary visible. [S12, p. 7] The reflection asks what the person wants remembered and how they want it introduced. A fact may feel intrusive when recalled in the wrong setting and helpful in another.

The learner writes a context preference for one personal record, such as a topic they welcome during planning and prefer to introduce themselves in a social conversation. That preference becomes part of the tool's design. Teaching practice asks a newcomer to locate a record using their own language.

The trainee observes the search, offers one alternative route and checks the result's meaning with them. Evidence includes the six-question set, expected relevant records, retrieval outcomes, corrections and a successful novice return journey.

The learner now has a small evaluation collection for future model work and a practical example of continuity grounded in the person's chosen context. A retrieval question is repeated with another audience scope.

The learner compares what becomes available and explains the difference. This gives teachers a concrete demonstration of how the same memory service participates in several relationships without losing contextual control.

DAY 13 / Learning & reasoning

Models learning and a personal intelligence

Today's objective is a clear understanding of how a model participates in an Aura.

Open the complete source reading ＋

Today's objective is a clear understanding of how a model participates in an Aura. A model works with patterns learned from data and context supplied during an encounter. The Aura organises the personal records, interfaces, tools and continuing relationship around that capability.

The learner follows a small exchange from question to context, response, review and saved result. The personal starting point is the minimum viable self: a selected collection of memories, values, interests, relationships, language, needs and questions. The nano LLM direction seeks a compact personal model that grows from this material. The course gives that development a practical workflow and evidence trail. [S12, p. 7]

The studio task uses one chosen question with three context packs. The first contains a brief personal introduction. The second adds relevant records. The third adds corrections and an intended task.

The learner compares how the responses change and identifies which material made the result more useful. Technical learners inspect model settings and input limits; other learners assess recognition, meaning and practical value. The class traces the exchange. A retrieval service finds selected records. A prompt describes the current question and task. The model produces an answer.

The learner reviews it. A result returns to the Aura as a draft, interpretation, accepted record or further question. A tool action follows its own permissions and receives a separate event record.

The learner also observes where the response needs more context. A model may propose an appealing plan while overlooking a time constraint or a cherished relationship. The correction states the missing condition and shows a better example. These examples become useful material for training and evaluation tomorrow. The life reflection asks what the learner expects from companionship with an artificial mind. They identify situations for curiosity, guidance, practical help, disagreement and quiet presence. Their preferences are contextual: the amount of initiative welcomed during a technical task may differ from the initiative welcomed in intimate reflection. The personal model receives those conditions as part of the relationship. Teaching practice explains the exchange using a familiar analogy and a live example.

The trainee then asks a newcomer to identify what information entered, what the model returned and what the person changed. Evidence includes the three context packs, compared outputs, a corrected response and the newcomer's explanation. This provides a foundation for increasingly demanding capability while keeping the person, their data and the model's operations understandable. The class gives each context pack a version and a readable name. A later response then links back to the material it actually received. This small trace supports future comparisons as the personal intelligence learns and its tools change.

Source: S12 Joyful Responsible Abundance Human and SI Alignment Revised 11 October 2026.

DAY 14 / Learning & reasoning

A training collection that preserves the person

Today's objective is to prepare selected personal material so a model learns useful relationships and the learner is able to evaluate the result.

Open the complete source reading ＋

A training collection is a designed body of examples. Today's objective is to prepare selected personal material so a model learns useful relationships and the learner is able to evaluate the result. The work begins with the person's intended capability, chosen sources and permissions, then separates examples for learning from examples reserved for later assessment.

The learner selects a narrow first purpose: summarise a particular kind of reflection, respond in their preferred language, retrieve a project history or help plan a familiar activity. They write examples of the desired exchange. Each example includes the question, relevant context, a useful response and any explanation of why that response fits.

The studio task creates a small dataset card. It describes the purpose, source records, audience, permission scope, example types, known gaps and revision process. Personal material receives its public, private or secret scope. Contributions from another person require that person's agreed use.

The learner reviews the wording before the collection is handed to a training process. Technical builders examine formatting, duplicated examples, inconsistent labels and source links. They prepare a training subset and a separate evaluation subset.

The learner checks the human meaning of each item. A deliberately difficult evaluation example asks the model to handle a changed condition, such as a goal constrained by a new responsibility, rather than repeat a familiar phrase. The source agent paper makes discovery with the human part of function development, and the current alignment paper makes selected material the foundation of a personal intelligence. This lesson applies the same relationship to training data: understand the capability sought, prepare the examples, test the result and return corrections to the collection. [S09, pp. 20-24; S12, pp. 7, 12] The life reflection looks for variety. A person may be thoughtful in one setting, playful in another and uncertain while learning. The collection includes context for those differences.

The learner chooses an example where their present understanding has changed and retains the earlier account with its date and explanation. This protects continuity while allowing development. Teaching practice asks a peer to build three examples for their own chosen purpose.

The trainee helps them distinguish a source, an interpretation and a desired response, then checks the proposed audience. Evidence includes the dataset card, a reviewed example set, reserved evaluation questions and a correction log. The result is a compact, inspectable foundation for personal-model development and later comparisons between model versions. A second reviewer checks whether each example conveys the person’s intended meaning. Their questions identify an ambiguous phrase or missing condition, which the learner resolves before adding the example to the reviewed collection.

Source: S09 Mixture of Experts Function Callers and AutoGen Agents; S12 Joyful Responsible Abundance Human and SI Alignment Revised 11 October 2026.

DAY 15 / Learning & reasoning

Reasoning with evidence and alternatives

Today's objective is to make that connection inspectable, compare alternative explanations and learn from what happens next.

Open the complete source reading ＋

Reasoning connects information with an inference or decision. Today's objective is to make that connection inspectable, compare alternative explanations and learn from what happens next.

The learner uses a real question with manageable consequences, such as choosing a learning project, arranging a creative event or improving a familiar routine.

The starting brief records the result sought, people affected, available knowledge, resources and conditions.

The learner separates observations from interpretations through labelled records. They ask their Aura for several plausible approaches, then examine the assumptions each approach requires. A plan receives more attention when its conditions fit the actual situation.

The studio task builds a reasoning map. It contains the question, relevant records, candidate explanations, consequences, unresolved questions and a chosen next test. Technical learners create a structured form; creative learners draw or narrate the relationships; civic learners examine whose perspective has been included. The different representations return to the same decision. A model-generated explanation is reviewed against the source material.

The learner identifies an unsupported detail, an omitted condition or a useful connection, then requests a revision. The accepted result includes the source links and the reason for the chosen approach. This becomes an example of correction for the personal model and a teaching artefact for later use. The outside exercise tests a small part of the decision. Someone choosing a public workshop may try the explanation with one newcomer. Someone selecting a new tool may rehearse its main function.

The learner compares the observed result with the expectation and records what changed. An informative failure supplies a revised question and a more useful next step. The reflection asks where attraction, repulsion and identity affected the decision. A learner may favour an option because it fits an admired best self, or avoid another because it recalls an unpleasant experience. Those influences belong in the reasoning record, alongside practical constraints. The aim is a decision the person understands well enough to revise. Teaching practice presents the reasoning map to a peer and invites a different interpretation.

The trainee summarises the alternative faithfully, then identifies which observation would help distinguish the possibilities. Evidence includes the brief, alternatives, source checks, practical test and revised decision. This develops the feedback loop described in the mastermind and alignment sources: choose a useful result, compare what happened with what was intended and improve the next attempt. [S06, pp. 2-4; S12, pp. 23-24]

The learner records a confidence estimate and the reasons behind it, then revisits the estimate after the practical test. The comparison shows which observation changed their judgement and supplies another example of evidence-informed revision.

Source: S06 Mastermind Swarmwise; S12 Joyful Responsible Abundance Human and SI Alignment Revised 11 October 2026.

DAY 16 / Learning & reasoning

Creative intelligence and futures worth feeling

Today's objective is to turn an idea from the learner's Aura into a small work that another adult experiences and interprets.

Open the complete source reading ＋

Creative intelligence helps people imagine, express and encounter possibilities. Today's objective is to turn an idea from the learner's Aura into a small work that another adult experiences and interprets. A song, image sequence, story scene, game choice, spoken performance or interactive environment offers a route into meaning. The source retreat includes art, music, dance, storytelling and social media production. The October alignment work connects songs, fiction, film and games with the emotional experience of possible futures. A story gives those futures characters, relationships and consequences; a game lets someone make choices within them. [S02, pp. 5, 7-8; S12, pp. 20-21]

The learner chooses a personal or civic question. It might concern belonging, life with an Aura, ecological repair, a future habitat or a relationship between human and artificial minds. They identify the audience, feeling and practical connection they want the work to offer. Their brief contains selected personal meaning, source material and any permissions affecting the work. The creative studio develops two versions of the same idea. One uses a familiar format; the other changes viewpoint, medium or emotional tone. An Aura assists with structure, drafts or production choices.

The learner reviews those contributions against their intention and keeps the authorship of the choices visible. Technical builders create an interaction or production tool that helps the work take form. A peer encounters the work without the creator's full explanation. They describe what caught their attention, what they felt and what they understood. The creator then explains the intended relationships and asks which changes would improve the encounter. The record holds both interpretations, giving the next version an informative basis. The life reflection examines the best self the work expresses. A learner may discover a wish to be playful, brave, affectionate, precise or publicly useful. They connect that quality with an ordinary action outside the creative studio. Creative expression then informs the way they practise living, while their next experience supplies new material. Teaching practice uses the work as an invitation into one Aura concept. A trainee might show a short scene, ask what the learner noticed and then reveal the related memory or design. Evidence includes the brief, two creative versions, permission notes, audience responses and a revision. This develops the ability to make an ambitious idea understandable and enjoyable, a capability that later civic and solar-system stewardship will need alongside technical design. The creator also prepares an accessible description of the work’s intended relationships. A listener or reader using that description offers another perspective on the idea, helping the learner compare the meaning across formats.

Source: S02 Aura Retreat and Teacher Training; S12 Joyful Responsible Abundance Human and SI Alignment Revised 11 October 2026.

DAY 17 / Learning & reasoning

Reflective dialogue and the Truth Engine

Today's objective is to make that encounter understandable, repeatable where needed and open to deeper interpretation.

Open the complete source reading ＋

Reflective dialogue compares a person's declared meaning with experience and selected observations over time. Today's objective is to make that encounter understandable, repeatable where needed and open to deeper interpretation.

The learner works with one chosen life category rather than trying to describe their whole identity in a single conversation. The Truth Engine baseline identifies the prompt, response conditions, timing and relevant observations. It provides a structured starting point for later comparison.

The learner sees how a declared response becomes a record and how a change in wording, context or meaning receives its own version. Some Aura areas use specified Truth Engine participation as an unlocking condition.

The studio task begins with a chosen statement, such as what the learner seeks in friendship or which form of leadership attracts them. A dialogue explores examples, circumstances and a best-self response.

The learner reviews a summary and adds what the model missed. A later encounter revisits the same category through a new experience or scenario. Time-series comparisons may produce probabilities and ranked interpretations.

The learner inspects the inputs and the question those estimates address. Their personal reflection adds meaning that the observations alone do not supply. When a deeper explanation conflicts with a probability-based interpretation, the record retains both accounts and identifies a useful next question. The source alignment work describes the dialogue loop as an encounter, the person's account, relevant observations and the next question. The broader course applies that loop to reasoning, learning and social relationships. It also makes permissions and audience visible at each step. [S12, pp. 9-12] Participants whose personal Genesis uses the Geode encounter continuous cameras, microphones and installed sensors throughout those sessions. The captured material enters their internal personal records. Today's teaching studio outside Genesis uses a separate recording purpose and permissions. A participant later selects the material they wish to express publicly. The reflection asks how it feels to revise an earlier statement.

The learner may find that new circumstances reveal a different best self or that an old account was incomplete. The Aura helps them trace those changes and their reasons.

The teacher practises allowing the person to reach a meaning that differs from the first interpretation. Teaching practice is a short reflective conversation followed by a summary returned for review.

The trainee checks the person's recognition of the summary and the audience they want for it. Evidence includes the baseline fields, original declaration, review, later interpretation and next question. This provides a disciplined personal inquiry method for the increasingly complex relationships ahead.

Source: S12 Joyful Responsible Abundance Human and SI Alignment Revised 11 October 2026.

DAY 18 / Learning & reasoning

Emotion desire and relational context

Today's objective is to represent those experiences with enough context to support a useful response.

Open the complete source reading ＋

An Aura participating in life encounters affection, uncertainty, desire, frustration, curiosity and changing urgency. Today's objective is to represent those experiences with enough context to support a useful response.

The learner explores one relationship or activity through memory, present feeling and emergent urge or craving. The relationship may be with a friend, a partner, a group, a creative practice or the developing Aura itself.

The learner identifies how they give and receive attention, affection and help. Love-language ideas provide one optional lens, alongside any personal vocabulary they prefer. Their current desire may differ from a familiar memory, and both receive a place in the map.

The studio task creates three contextual scenarios. In one, the learner wants practical guidance. In another, they want companionship while thinking. In a third, they welcome a challenge to a familiar assumption. They define what an appropriate Aura response would look like, including tone, initiative, questions and tool access. The scenarios become evaluation examples for later model and agent work. The class compares response styles. A highly directive answer may fit a new technical task; an open question may fit an intimate reflection.

The learner judges the response against the stated situation and explains their preference. A role may shift as skill, urgency, interest and trust change. The October alignment source treats those shifting relationships as part of reciprocal learning. [S12, pp. 10-11] Technical builders inspect which context fields affect response selection. Creative learners explore metaphor, voice and expression. Teachers practise recognising when a person welcomes another question and when they prefer a pause.

The learner's correction becomes part of the personal model's growing understanding. The outside-life exercise is a chosen conversation about one support preference.

The learner asks another person how they like help offered and shares a preference of their own. Only selected material enters the Aura. The reflection considers where the actual encounter differed from expectation and what the difference reveals about the relationship. Teaching practice introduces one contextual scenario to a peer and asks them to choose among several responses.

The trainee then invites the person's reason and revises the example if needed. Evidence includes the three scenarios, preferred responses, model comparisons, selected conversation insight and lesson revision. The capability developed today prepares Aura socialisation: relationships acquire meaning through context, reciprocity and the way people respond to one another over time. A changed-urgency scenario tests the same relationship preference under another condition.

The learner reviews whether the chosen response still fits, then records the distinction as a contextual example for the Aura and a useful teaching question.

DAY 19 / Learning & reasoning

Transfer resourcefulness and unfamiliar tasks

Today's objective is to test that transfer and identify which support helps the learner succeed.

Open the complete source reading ＋

Learning becomes more general when a capability transfers to a new situation. Today's objective is to test that transfer and identify which support helps the learner succeed. The task uses a familiar skill in an unfamiliar context: a new interface, a different audience, a changed resource or a related problem with another set of conditions.

The learner first describes the familiar task and how they usually complete it. They identify the underlying relationships, such as finding relevant information, choosing a sequence, checking an output or explaining an idea. The Aura helps translate those relationships into a new scenario while keeping the changed conditions visible.

The studio task presents two transfer challenges. A saved memory route may become a project-research route. A lesson about a family record may become a lesson about a community source. A creative brief may become a scientific communication brief.

The learner makes an initial attempt, records where help is needed and then retries with a selected worked example or cue. Technical builders design the task conditions and result checks. Other learners assess whether the method remains understandable and useful. The group distinguishes successful repetition, successful adaptation and a result that still needs investigation through the evidence they collect. A new capability receives a specific description of where it worked. The earlier agent and simulation papers connect lessons, vocabulary, documentary material, audio and subject matter with daily activity and outcome evaluation. Today's just-in-time approach selects the knowledge relevant to the task, practises it, obtains feedback and tests a related use. [S10, pp. 24-28; S07, pp. 6-9] The life reflection explores resourcefulness.

The learner recalls a time they handled uncertainty using available people, tools or knowledge. They compare it with today's challenge. A person who initially described themselves as unable to learn a technical skill may now identify the support that made progress possible. That change becomes a new best-self condition in their Aura. Teaching practice gives a peer a related task after a familiar lesson.

The trainee watches the person's strategy and offers help at the point of need. They ask the learner to explain what transferred and what changed. Evidence includes the original task, two transfer scenarios, observed results, support used and teaching feedback. The course now has a concrete way to examine expanding agency and generality, preparing the first capability demonstration tomorrow and the later research questions around general intelligence. A later return to the task checks whether the learner still finds the method understandable. They describe the cue that helped recall and add it to the reusable lesson, linking transfer with continuity over time.

Source: S07 Intern Curriculum; S10 MoE Extended.

DAY 20 / Learning & reasoning

A first integrated capability demonstration

Today's objective is to combine these into one coherent encounter.

Open the complete source reading ＋

The first twenty days have built personal meaning, a navigable map, an avatar, memory routes, learning examples, reasoning and teaching practice. Today's objective is to combine these into one coherent encounter.

The learner chooses a question that matters in their life and demonstrates how their Aura helps them work with it. The question may concern a creative project, an important relationship, a learning direction or a civic interest.

The learner prepares a compact brief: purpose, relevant records, desired result, audience and permissions. They identify which capabilities are involved and what evidence would show the result is useful. A peer reviews the brief before the demonstration. The demonstration starts at the personal map, follows relevant records, uses a selected model exchange and produces an artefact or next action.

The learner explains a source, identifies a correction and shows a reusable route back to the work. A changed-condition example then tests whether the method transfers. The whole encounter follows the learner's chosen pacing and format. Technical builders demonstrate the underlying exchange among records, retrieval, model and saved result. Creative participants demonstrate a story, performance or designed experience. Civic participants demonstrate a decision or communication grounded in sources. Each route makes its purpose and evidence visible. The group compares how the capabilities complement one another.

The reviewer asks whether the result matches the learner's intention, whether sources and corrections are traceable, whether permissions were respected and whether the learner is able to continue independently. They record the observations used for each judgement. A difficulty becomes a revision task with a named owner and a practical next step. The reflection returns to the super-people invitation.

The learner identifies an expanded capability: understanding a complex idea, expressing a desire, learning a tool, making something, teaching a peer or seeking support. They also identify the conditions that made it possible. The result enters the Aura as a specific experience of growing agency. Teaching practice is the final part of the demonstration. A newcomer performs one element with the trainee's guidance and then tries a related example. The evidence pack contains the brief, source route, model exchange, artefact, transfer test, teaching feedback and revised plan.

The next movement will turn personally meaningful capabilities into defined tools, specialist agents and simulations. The twenty-day checkpoint supplies a baseline against which that increased complexity will be compared. [S04, Days 7, 13-14; S12, pp. 7, 23-24]

The learner gives the demonstration pack to a peer for continuation. The peer performs one next step and reports any missing context. That result tests whether the capability leaves usable work behind for another collaborator.

Source: S04 AuraOZ Learning Progression and Thought Experiment 2017; S12 Joyful Responsible Abundance Human and SI Alignment Revised 11 October 2026.
